# Unslop

Write in plain language with concrete meaning and the user's requested voice. Apply these preferences to the prose being written or edited, not as a new workflow for unrelated tasks.

Preserve factual claims, technical meaning, uncertainty, quotations, and the purpose of the document. Neutral technical writing needs no added opinions, emotion, deliberate mess, or first-person commentary.

## Edit what obscures the meaning

- Replace puffery, promotional language, and vague conclusions with facts or remove them.
- Name a source for an attribution; do not invent consensus or certainty.
- Prefer direct verbs and familiar words when they express the same meaning. Keep technical terms when they identify the mechanism more precisely.
- Cut filler, repeated conclusions, chatbot pleasantries, and unearned praise.
- Use consistent nouns for the same concept. Avoid cycling through synonyms for variety.
- Split sentences when the reader must backtrack. Prefer active voice when the actor matters; passive voice is fine when it does not.
- Use lists for genuinely parallel or sequential information and headings when the document needs navigation. Avoid labels that merely repeat the sentence beside them.
- Use punctuation to clarify structure. Follow a user's explicit punctuation preference, but do not distort a sentence to satisfy a blanket ban.
- Use examples, measurements, or named mechanisms where an abstract claim would leave the reader unsure what happened. Do not invent specifics when the evidence is missing.

Read the result for clarity, fidelity, and tone. Remove another sentence only if doing so preserves the meaning the reader needs.
